% Abstract, reframed for the Robin extension.

\begin{abstract}
We extend the neural bootstrapping method (NBM) to elliptic problems with Robin
boundary conditions on irregular interfaces in three spatial dimensions. NBM trains a
neural network surrogate for the solution of a PDE by minimising the residuals of a
finite discretisation evaluated on implicit Cartesian cells centred at collocation
points, thereby inheriting the accuracy and convergence properties of the underlying
numerical method while restricting optimisation to first order automatic
differentiation. In the Robin setting the equation is posed on the interior region
alone: no jump conditions arise, a single network represents the solution, and the
boundary condition is imposed through surface integrals over the interface within the
cells it crosses, using a first-order Taylor projection onto the interface.

We report convergence for three geometries -- a sphere, a star, and the union of two
stars -- across four refinement levels, and observe first-order convergence in both
the root-mean-squared and $L^{\infty}$ error norms, consistent with the order of the
interface projection and with the rates reported for the original formulation.

In the course of this work we identify a failure mode of practical significance for
residual-minimisation solvers of this type. Because the finite-volume residual is
Jacobi-preconditioned, its sensitivity to the solution error carries a power of the
grid spacing; at fine resolutions the resulting loss gradients fall to the magnitude
of the numerical-stability constant $\varepsilon$ in the Adam optimiser. The optimiser
then loses its scale invariance and stalls at a solution-error floor that grows under
refinement, so that error increases rather than decreases with resolution. Reducing
$\varepsilon$ below the gradient scale removes the floor and restores monotone
convergence, improving the solution error at the finest resolution by a factor of
$18.7$ at no additional computational cost. We note that error growth at the finest
resolution is visible in previously published results for this class of solver.

The algorithms are implemented in JAX-DIPS
(\url{https://github.com/JAX-DIPS/JAX-DIPS}).
\end{abstract}
