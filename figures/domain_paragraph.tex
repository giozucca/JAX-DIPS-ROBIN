The three test geometries are of intrinsically different size: a sphere of radius
$0.5$, a star of base radius $1.183$, and the union of two stars of base radius $0.8$
centred at $(\mp 0.5,\, \pm 0.5,\, \mp 0.5)$. The edge length $L$ is therefore not a
free parameter but follows from the geometry. Two requirements fix it. First, the
finite-volume stencil samples the solution one cell either side of each grid point,
so $\Omega$ must enclose $\Omega^{-}$ with a clearance of at least one cell at the
coarsest resolution considered. Second, grid points lying far outside $\Omega^{-}$
generate equations that involve only the exterior network and cannot constrain the
solution in $\Omega^{-}$; enlarging $\Omega$ beyond the required clearance therefore
expends resolution without improving accuracy. Taking $L$ as the smallest cube
satisfying the first requirement gives $L = 2.0$, $3.6$ and $4.2$ respectively, with
a clearance of approximately $0.5$ in each case (Table~\ref{tab:domains}).

A consequence is that the grid spacing
\begin{equation}
    \Delta x = \frac{L}{N_{x} - 1}
    \label{eq:spacing}
\end{equation}
is not equal across geometries at fixed $N_{x}$. Convergence is therefore assessed
independently for each geometry, and absolute errors are compared only within a
geometry.
